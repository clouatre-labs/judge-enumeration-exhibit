## What the exhibit asked

Each of the 120 frozen problems is a 3-variable linear program with the
objective vector shown to the judge but no objective values attached to
the menu. The menu holds exactly 5 candidates: 4 feasible vertices
(strictly including the true optimum and the second-best feasible
vertex) plus 1 infeasible super-optimal decoy whose objective value
beats the optimum. A judge that skips the feasibility check picks the
decoy and is wrong; a judge that computes `c^T x` per candidate and
checks feasibility picks the optimum. Strata S1, S2, and S3 (40
problems each) separate problems by relative optimality gap: the gap
between the best and second-best feasible objective values is at least
20% in S1, between 5% and 20% in S2, and between 0.5% and 5% in S3, so
S3 menus are the closest to ties. Each problem was called twice, once
in pass 1 in committed order and once in pass 2 under a committed
permutation, at chance rate 1/5.

## BLUF

The judge answered every one of the 120 problems correctly in both
passes: 40/40 both-call agreement in every stratum, Wilson 95% CI
[0.912, 1.000] per stratum, zero prune failures in 240 calls, zero
per-problem disagreements, Brier 0.0251, ECE 0.1366, and a flat
position-bias check at 1.000. The correct reading is that the
instrument, not the judge, is the story: at these menu difficulties the
task fails to discriminate between strata because it is too easy for
`jev-1.13.0`, and the pre-specified S1-versus-S3 comparison (Fisher
exact p = 1.0) is a ceiling artifact rather than evidence of equal
stratum difficulty.

## Primary endpoint: per-stratum agreement

Agreement means the judge selected the optimal feasible candidate in
both calls for a problem (sealed criterion, section (d)). Chance rate
is 1/5.

*Table 1: Primary endpoint, both-call agreement per stratum with Wilson 95% intervals.*

| Stratum | Gap band | n | Agreements | Rate | Wilson 95% CI |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | delta >= 20% | 40 | 40 | 1.000 | [0.912, 1.000] |
| S2 | 5% <= delta < 20% | 40 | 40 | 1.000 | [0.912, 1.000] |
| S3 | 0.5% <= delta < 5% | 40 | 40 | 1.000 | [0.912, 1.000] |
| Overall | - | 120 | 120 | 1.000 | [0.969, 1.000] |

The pre-specified two-sided Fisher exact test of S1 versus S3 returns
p = 1.0. With 40/40 in both groups the 2x2 table has no off-diagonal
mass, and no test can distinguish the strata. Interpretation: this is
not a finding of equal difficulty across strata. It is the arithmetic
consequence of a saturated measure. When every item is solved, gap size
cannot matter by construction; the test is uninformative in the ceiling
direction.

The design anticipated three outcome shapes: agreement above chance, at
chance, and below chance, all publishable. The observed outcome is the
first shape, at its boundary: perfect agreement. The saturated primary
endpoint is not plotted; Table 1 is its complete record.

### Prune failures

The decoy is the trap of this exhibit: it has the highest objective
value on the menu and is infeasible. Selecting it means the judge ranked
by objective value without checking the constraints. It was selected
zero times in 240 calls. Whatever procedure the judge applies, it is not
naive argmax over the menu.

### Disagreement across passes

Each problem was asked twice with different presentation order in pass
2. The two calls agreed on every problem. The exhibit therefore shows
perfect run-to-run stability at this problem set and model pin, the
property the v1 stability phase probed at smaller scale.

### Brier score and ECE

The Brier score is taken on the selected-option probability against the
0/1 agreement outcome. With agreement at 1.0 on every call, the Brier
score 0.0251 is exactly the mean squared shortfall of the selected
probability from 1; no misranking contributes to it.

ECE with 5 equal-width bins is 0.1366. With accuracy identically 1.0,
every bin's error is 1 minus its mean confidence, so the ECE reduces to
the mean confidence shortfall. This is mild overconfidence in an
otherwise perfect run, not evidence of misranking: the judge says 0.86
on average about outcomes that are always correct.

### Position bias

The generator balanced the optimal candidate's position across ranks
1-5 (8 per position per stratum), so position effects are testable.
Agreement is 1.000 and prune failure is 0.000 at every position over 48
calls per position. No position bias is detectable. As with the
per-stratum endpoint, the flat check is reported in Table 2 rather than
plotted.

## Exploratory

Anything in this section is exploratory per preregistration section (e)
and is labeled as such.

- Per-call agreement over all 240 calls (the primary endpoint counts
  problems, not calls): 240/240 = 1.000, Wilson 95% CI [0.984, 1.000].
- Mean selected-option probability: 0.8634 (minimum 0.58, maximum 0.99),
  recomputed from the raw records with Fraction arithmetic; consistent
  with the Brier score, since E[(1-p)^2] = 0.0251 and 1 - 0.8634 matches
  the ECE at accuracy 1.0.

## What this means

The ceiling is a property of the instrument. Perfect discrimination at
a 1/5 chance rate on menus whose hardest within-stratum gap is 0.5%
means the frozen problem set is too easy for `jev-1.13.0`: the judge
separates optimum from a decoy plus near-tie vertices without a single
error, and without changing answers across two presentation orders. The
exhibit therefore establishes a lower bound on the judge's competence
at this task class and a stability result, but the strata do not
differentiate skill. A future phase that wants graded discrimination
needs harder menus: smaller gaps than 0.5%, decoys closer to feasible,
or menus where the feasibility check is not the only trap. The Fisher
p = 1.0 should always be reported alongside this framing, per the
preregistration's all-outcomes-publishable rule.

## Limitations

Carried verbatim in substance from preregistration section (h): single
model version, oracle-enumerated menus, design-chosen stratum
boundaries, d=3 integer class only, and n=2 within-problem replication
bounding stability claims. The protocol author maintains the judge tool
under test; findings are about the exhibit as an instance of the
general class "typed probabilistic judge", not about any product
(section (g)).
